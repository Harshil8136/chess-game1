# @oltrem/email: Write: The template picker, A letter, A design, Fields, Write in HTML, The composer's HTML, Preview, Address suggestions and more and more

> Part of [the @oltrem/email README](../README.md).

## Write, continued

| Part | What it does |
|---|---|
| The template picker | In the tools row, every template for Write in one select, letters first and the most used next, after **No template**, with its Use button, which posts the form as it is (`?intent=template`, `pick`), so nothing typed is lost; with the script, picking one presses Use. `?template=<id>` opens Write with one picked (the template page's **Write with it**). A template that holds code is shown locked, with why, to someone without `email.write.code` |
| A letter | Its subject and words go in the form to be changed: into an empty box, or in place of the last letter's words when they were not changed; words the person wrote are kept, with the letter's under them. **No template** takes unchanged letter words away again |
| A design | The message goes in its message place, with the address's signature; a design with no message place shows no box and goes as it is |
| Fields | `{{first_name}}`, `{{last_name}}`, `{{name}}` and `{{email}}` come from the person each email goes to; each other field the template names is a line to fill in (`field.<name>`, 300 characters, 30 at most), and a field it does not name is dropped. A value is escaped before it goes into markup |
| Write in HTML | For someone who holds `email.write.code` at the company: a Words or HTML choice. HTML is cleaned by `sanitizeEmailHtml` before it is kept or sent, and its text copy is read out of it. Its `<style>` rules are also written onto each tag they reach (`inlineStyles`: the tag's own style wins, then a more specific rule over a less specific one, an id over a class over a tag, then a later rule over an earlier one, `@media` stays in the head; until 2026-11-11 only the order counted; since then each declaration is written once, `oncePerDeclaration`, so cleaning the email again as a draft save and then the send do gives the same email, not a longer one, while a property given twice with two values stays as the author's fallback (a plain colour before a gradient Outlook cannot read) and a `;` inside `url(...)`, quotes or `&quot;` is never a split, and a fragment without its own `<body>` has its rules written on too), because many mail apps read only a tag's own style; and a whole pasted email (its own `<html>` or `<body>`, `isDocument`) is sent as its own page with the signature at the foot of its body, never inside the plain letter, which is what broke pasted emails in Gmail and Outlook |
| The composer's HTML | Since 2026-11-08 `POST /write` and `POST /written` take `editor: 'rich'` with `format: 'html'`: HTML made by Write's composer, which anyone who may send everyday mail may write. `cleanRich` keeps only the composer's tags (lines, bold, italic, underline, strike, lists, quotes, three headings, links, pictures, a rule) and a few chosen looks (colour, background colour, size, weight, style, decoration, alignment), and writes each tag's look on the tag itself; everything else goes |
| Preview | Words with no template: with the composer (the page script) the words show as they will look in the box itself; without it, a Preview part under the form draws them with their marks as they are typed (`data-marks-of`), from the server's copy at first. With a template or HTML, **Preview** saves the draft and shows it framed, as `GET /written/:id/preview` renders it: the template at the version written with, the fields and the signature filled in, under a policy that runs nothing. A sent email written with a template or in HTML opens the same way |
| Address suggestions | The browser offers the addresses the person wrote to lately (the last forty emails sent) as they type To, Cc or Bcc |
| Save as template | For someone who may build templates: the subject and words become a new letter named after the subject, and the email is kept as a draft. Only words, never HTML |

| Part | What it does |
|---|---|
| From | The addresses the person may send everyday mail from (`email.senders.use` at the address's place), the defaults first; a dropdown under the line (the kit's `ui-drop`): each address with its name, **Default** on the default, a tick on the one picked, and **Another address…** at its foot. With none, the page says so and sends them to Senders. **Another address** (`?intent=add-from`, `newEmail`, `newName`; `POST /senders/own`) adds one there and then |
| To, Cc, Bcc | Typed as a mail app takes them: separated by commas, semicolons or lines, each a bare address or `Name <address>` (`readAddresses`); with the script each becomes a chip (red when it is not an address), Backspace takes the last back, and the form posts the same comma-separated line. An empty Cc or Bcc is folded away until it is opened. What is not an address is named, and the form comes back as typed. Fifty people at most in all; for more, the page points to a campaign. With several people in To each gets an email of their own addressed to them alone, so nobody sees who else it went to, and the name fields of a template are that person's; Cc and Bcc go with the first person's email only. A reply in a conversation stays one email, since its people are already on it |
| Subject and message | The message is plain words with three marks: `**bold**`, `_italic_` and a line starting with `- ` for a list; an https address becomes a link. It is sent as a plain letter in HTML (`writtenHtml`, everything typed escaped) and as text, under the built-in template `email.written` |
| Sending | `POST /write` checks the address may be used and queues the message like any other: the lease, the failover and the delivery reports are the same. It is audited as `email.write` with the number of recipients (and the time, for one scheduled), never the words. Each person's own email is a message of its own: the first keeps the email's message key and the others take `<key>.<n>`, so Undo takes back every one while they wait, and Sent shows what became of the first |
| Undo | A sent email waits ten seconds (`UNDO_SECONDS`), or what the Owner set in `email.undo_seconds` (5 to 30), in the queue before it is handed over. The page says so, to whom it goes, with an Undo button that takes it back into the form as a draft (`POST /written/:id/undo`, audited as `email.unsend`) and a link to it on Sent; Sent has the same Undo while it is in the queue (`/email/sent?intent=undo`). Once the queue has handed it over, Undo answers `KIT-E-1511` |
| Save draft | Keeps what is typed, even half done, as the person's draft (`POST /written`); the page Email, Drafts lists them, opens one into the form and deletes one. As Gmail does, the form saves itself three seconds after the last change and before the page is left (the kit's `data-keep-draft`, posting `?intent=autosave`, which answers in JSON): the bar says Saving and Saved where the Save draft button was, the address becomes `?draft=<id>` so a reload or Back reopens it, an address still being typed waits until it is whole, and Send waits for a save under way. Without the page script the Save draft button stays |
| Files | Since 2026-11-08 (the client's point 8): under the message, **Attach a file** saves the email as a draft and attaches the file to it (`?intent=attach`; with the script, choosing the file is enough), and each file shows as a chip with its size and a button that takes it off (`?intent=detach&file=`). Ten files and 10 MB attached together at most (`FILES_MAX`, `FILES_MAX_BYTES`); a file over 10 MB, up to 95 MB (`DOWNLOAD_MAX_BYTES`), is not attached but listed at the end of the email as a link to download that works for 30 days (`DOWNLOAD_DAYS`, `withDownloads`), so it goes through any provider; anyone the email reaches can open that link until then. With the script and a saved draft, a chosen file goes straight to the draft's route (`data-upload`, the kit's frame script), so a large file never passes through the page's form; of the kinds an email may carry (`FILE_TYPES`: PDF, Word, Excel, PowerPoint, OpenDocument, RTF, CSV, text, calendar, JPG, PNG, GIF and ZIP; anything else is refused with `KIT-E-1512`). Every person's own email carries the files. They are kept in the client's one bucket under `mail/attachments/`, and each email sent gets a link of its own per file, good for a day (`FILE_LINK_HOURS`), from which the provider downloads it during the call (`GET /hooks/email/files/<token>`, a download that runs nothing), so the Worker never reads a file. Only Brevo and Resend fetch files from a link (the kit's `attachmentBytes`): Write offers files only when one of them sends, an email with files goes only through one of them, never through the Cloudflare route, and Send is refused in words when none could take them. A file whose email is deleted goes within the hour (`email.files`) |
| Before it goes | Since 2026-11-11 (`before-send.ts`): Send and Schedule ask once when an address in To, Cc or Bcc is one slip from a large free mail service (`gmial.com`, `.con`: `likelyAddress`, the lists' own rule) or when the subject or the message speaks of a file (attached, attachment, enclosed, adjunto, anexo, in their forms) and the draft has none (the form's `files` count). Quoted lines, a forwarded email and a reply's or forward's subject are not read. It also asks once when the email reads like a promotion, which Gmail puts in its Promotions tab whatever the setup: two signs or more of an offer in the subject or the hidden preview line, a newsletter build (pictures, buttons, a hidden preview line), Unsubscribe or View in browser links, six links or more, and shouting (a row of three exclamation or question marks, or a line of two words or more in capital letters) (`promotionSigns`, which the campaign page reads too); the words name each sign and the way to Primary (a short letter in Words from a person's name) or to send it as a campaign from a separate address. A link that goes nowhere (empty, `#`, a template's yourwebsite.com) is named too. The page says what it saw at the top and offers **Correct and send** (`fix=1`: each such address becomes the one meant) or **Send as typed** / **Send without a file** / **Send anyway**; each posts with `?sure=1`, which asks nothing again. No query and no provider call |
| The To box | Since 2026-11-11 (the kit's frame script): a list pasted from a spreadsheet, a signature or another email becomes one chip per address (commas, semicolons, tabs, new lines, or spaces between bare addresses part them; `Name <address>` and `Name address` stay whole; `mailto:` and quotes go); the same address twice is one chip; a bare address the person wrote to before gets the name it was sent with. The suggestions under To, Cc and Bcc are the people the person wrote to in their last 100 emails sent, the most often first, then the newest (`written.recent`) |
| Leaving with words typed | The form is `data-keep-typed`: once something is typed, a link out opens the page in full so the browser asks before the page goes; a form drawn again after a refusal counts as typed. Sending or saving asks nothing |
| Sent | The page Email, Sent lists the emails the person sent from Write, newest first, from the moment Send is pressed: the subject and first words, to whom, when, and what became of each in a word (Undo window, Queued, Waiting, Retrying, Sending, Failed, Sent, Delivered, Opened, Clicked, Delayed, Bounced, Refused, Marked as spam), read from its message's row and the provider's reports. One held for want of an email service says so in plain words, and the list looks again by itself (`data-live`) every five seconds while one is in its Undo window or being handed over, every thirty while one waits, and not at all when nothing moves. One opens with its people, its words and its timeline (sending started, the delivery outcome, first open, first click, a spam report, the provider's words when it failed), and **Forward** (subject `Fwd:`, the email quoted below, To empty) and **Send again** (the same people and words), each a new email in the form |
| Signature | Each sender address keeps one (Email settings, Addresses, Edit; migration `0053`), set by whoever manages the address: plain words with the same marks; written in Write's composer, with its formatting, or a designed signature pasted as HTML code (from an AI chat too: the code alone is taken), cleaned (`sanitizeEmailHtml`, `inlineStyles`) and kept as HTML behind the mark `SIGNATURE_HTML`, which only the server writes (`keptSignature`); a designed one opens as code to paste over. 20,000 characters at most once cleaned (1,000 until then). An email written from the address carries it under the message, in grey in the HTML and after `-- ` in the text, taken as it is when the email is queued; the email's own words, and so Forward and Send again, stay without it |
| Send later | A day and a time on the company's clock (the client's `timeZone`, the bookings time zone in the template; UTC when none), at least a minute ahead and within a year. The job `email.written` queues each one within fifteen minutes of its time, with the queue's own wait for the rest, so it goes on time. The job runs every five minutes while any email is scheduled; with none it sleeps (an hour a run), and Send later wakes it. Drafts, Scheduled lists them; Take back makes one a draft again |
