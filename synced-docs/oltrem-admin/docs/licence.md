# The licence in plain words

> **In plain words.** The kit is **source-available, not open source**: you can read it, run it
> and build on it within the rules below, but you cannot change its code without asking. The
> legal text is [`LICENSE`](../LICENSE), the Oltrem Licence, version 1.0. This page explains
> it; where the two differ, the licence wins.

## What you may do

| You are | You may |
|---|---|
| A person using it for yourself, with no money involved | use it freely |
| An organisation with less than one million US dollars of revenue a year (income, for a non-profit, school or public body), counting parent and sister companies | use it freely, including in your business and to run things for your own customers |
| An organisation at or above that line | use it with a **Commercial Licence**: a separate paid agreement with the licensor. If you cross the line, you have ninety days to arrange one or stop |

Configuring the kit, creating your own client repository from its template, writing your own
settings and your own modules against the kit's documented interfaces: all of that is **use**,
and allowed, as long as none of the kit's own files is changed.

## What needs permission

Changing the kit's code, files or documentation, in any way and for any purpose, even on your
own computer, needs the licensor's written permission first. The change is sent in, reviewed,
and granted, refused or granted with conditions; no answer means no. A permitted change may be
taken into the kit for everyone. The only exceptions are the rights your country's law gives
every lawful user and no licence can remove, such as studying the program; the licence names
them.

You may pass on complete, unchanged copies with the licence attached. You may not sell copies,
and you may not pass on a changed copy.

## How to ask

Permission to change the code and Commercial Licences are requested at the contact address
published on oltrem.com or in the kit's README. Pricing is not in the licence; it is agreed per
Commercial Licence, so it can change without the licence changing.

## Who this binds

Anyone who gets the kit from its repositories or packages. A client the licensor runs the kit
for has a service agreement with the licensor; that agreement, not this licence, governs them.
